# SiPM and Charge Amplifier Readout

## 1. Purpose
Theoretical background of the Silicon Photomultiplier (SiPM) and its readout using a charge‑sensitive amplifier.

---
## 2. Definitions

### 2.1 Silicon Photomultiplier (SiPM) Operation

When a photon is absorbed in a microcell, it may trigger an avalanche process, producing a fixed amount of charge. The charge generated by a single avalanche event is given by:

$$Q_{1ph} = G \cdot q_e$$

**Where:**
* $G$: SiPM gain (number of charge carriers generated during one avalanche event).
* $q_e$: Elementary charge ($1.602 \times 10^{-19}$ C).

> **Note:** The gain $G$ is typically provided in the device datasheet and depends on the applied overvoltage.

For multiple detected photons:

$$Q_{total} = N \cdot G \cdot q_e$$

**Where:**
* $N$: Number of triggered microcells (approximately equal to detected photons under low light conditions).

Thus, the SiPM behaves as a quantized charge generator.

---

### 2.2 SiPM Capacitance
Since all microcells are connected in parallel, the SiPM exhibits a total equivalent capacitance ($C_{SiPM}$). This capacitance arises from the junction capacitance of each microcell and the parallel combination of all cells.

$C_{SiPM}$ influences:
* Bandwidth
* Noise
* Stability of the front-end amplifier

In a charge amplifier configuration, however, the gain does not depend directly on $C_{SiPM}$.

---

### 2.3 Microcell Recharge Time Constant
After an avalanche event, each microcell must recharge through its internal quench resistor. This follows exponential recovery behavior characterized by the microcell time constant:

$$\tau_{cell} = R_q \cdot C_{cell}$$

**Where:**
* $R_q$: Internal quench resistor.
* $C_{cell}$: Microcell capacitance.

> **Note:** The gain $\tau_{cell}$ is typically provided in the device datasheet.

The value of $\tau_{cell}$ affects the maximum count rate, pulse shape, and dynamic response.

---

### 2.4 Charge Amplifier Principle
To convert the SiPM charge pulse into a measurable voltage, a charge-sensitive amplifier (CSA) is used. In an ideal charge amplifier:

$$V_{out} = \frac{Q_{in}}{C_f}$$

Substituting the SiPM charge expression:

$$V_{out} = \frac{N \cdot G \cdot q_e}{C_f}$$

**This shows that:**
1. The output voltage is proportional to the number of detected photons.
2. The gain is determined only by the feedback capacitor $C_f$.
3. The detector capacitance $C_{SiPM}$ does not set the signal amplitude.

---

### 2.5 Feedback Capacitor ($C_f$)
In a charge-sensitive amplifier (CSA), the output voltage is determined by the input charge and the feedback capacitance:

$$V_{out} = \frac{Q_{in}}{C_f}$$

For an SiPM, the input charge generated by $N$ detected photons is:

$$Q_{in} = N \cdot G \cdot q_e$$

Combining both equations:

$$V_{out} = \frac{N \cdot G \cdot q_e}{C_f}$$

Rearranging to determine the required feedback capacitance for a specific target voltage:

$$C_f = \frac{N \cdot G \cdot q_e}{V_{out}}$$

**Thus, the feedback capacitor is selected based on:**
* Expected maximum number of detected photons ($N$).
* SiPM gain ($G$) from the datasheet.
* Desired output voltage amplitude ($V_{out}$).

The value of $C_f$ directly sets the charge-to-voltage conversion gain of the system.

---

### 2.6 Feedback Resistor ($R_f$)
The feedback resistor determines the discharge rate of the feedback capacitor and therefore the pulse decay time. The decay time constant of the charge amplifier is:

$$\tau = R_f \cdot C_f$$

To ensure accurate charge integration, the amplifier time constant is chosen relative to the SiPM microcell recharge time constant ($\tau_{cell}$):

$$\tau \ge k \cdot \tau_{cell}$$

**Where:**
* $\tau_{cell}$ is provided in the datasheet.
* $k$ is typically selected between 3 and 5.

Solving for the feedback resistor:

$$R_f = \frac{\tau}{C_f}$$

Therefore, once $C_f$ is determined and the desired shaping time $\tau$ is selected, the required feedback resistor can be calculated.

---

### 2.7 Design Considerations
* **$C_f$**: Defines signal amplitude and dynamic range. Larger capacitance prevents saturation but lowers sensitivity.
* **$R_f$**: Defines pulse width and maximum count rate capability. Lower resistance allows for faster counting but may increase electronic noise.
* Both parameters must be selected considering noise, bandwidth, and stability constraints of the operational amplifier.

---

### 2.8 Detector Shot Noise (SiPM)
The dominant intrinsic noise mechanism of a SiPM is shot noise associated with dark current. The RMS shot noise current is given by:

$$i_{shot,rms} = \sqrt{2q I_{dark} \Delta f}$$

**Where:**
* $q$: Elementary charge ($1.602 \times 10^{-19} \text{ C}$).
* $I_{dark}$: Detector dark current.
* $\Delta f$: Effective noise bandwidth.

Shot noise arises from the discrete nature of charge carriers and follows Poisson statistics.

**Key Properties:**
* Shot noise is proportional to the square root of dark current.
* Shot noise increases with bandwidth.
* Shot noise is statistically independent from electronic amplifier noise.

This contribution must be included in the total noise RSS calculation when modeling full system performance.
